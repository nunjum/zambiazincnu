Assignment Iteration 2

Project Trace: Zinc Asymmetry Framework

Tracing zinc, and the lead found alongside it, from Kabwe, Zambia to its end uses, and asking who earns and who absorbs the harm. Argument: demand for transition metals rewards re-extraction of toxic waste while children pay the health cost.

Scope. Kabwe's poisoning is mainly lead. Zinc is the co-product that makes reprocessing the waste piles profitable. Health figures below are about lead; price and trade figures are about zinc.

Evidence tags: SAYARI Sayari trade/ownership data · WEB public source (URL in the log) · ALLEGED claimed, disputed or unanswered · UNVERIFIED not in the data pack.

Timeline: four epochs, c. 1902 – 2026

From a colonial-era lead-zinc mine, through closure and partial remediation, to permitted re-extraction of the waste and a child-rights complaint now before the African Union.

  1. Epoch 1 · c. 1902 – 1994

    Lead-zinc mining at Kabwe

    • The mine operated from roughly 1902, 1904 or 1906 until 1994; sources differ on the start year, so the range is shown. ZCCM owned it at closure. WEB HRW
    • Anglo American: a class action on behalf of about 140,000 women and children alleges its Kabwe operations from 1925 to 1974 contributed to the pollution. Anglo denies causing current conditions. ALLEGED / DISPUTED WEB BHRRC
  2. Epoch 2 · 1994 – 2024

    Closure, legacy waste and partial remediation

    • About 6.4 million tonnes of uncovered waste remain. WEB HRW
    • Dec 2016: the World Bank approves ZMERIP, a US$65.6M IDA credit (a loan). It funded testing, treatment and hotspot remediation but did not remove the source. Project end date: UNVERIFIED WEB World Bank
    • 2016: an adult scavenger told the Global Press Journal he sold 25 kg sacks of waste for 50 kwacha (about US$5). WEB See the ledger.
  3. Epoch 3 · 2025

    Re-extraction of the waste

    • March 2025: HRW documents waste mining and processing by South African, Chinese and local firms. "Black Mountain" is the Enviro Processing Ltd concession. Zambia has designated Kabwe zinc and lead as "critical minerals". WEB HRW
    • Miners say they sold to Datong Industries, Chengde Mining and Superdeal Investments. The companies did not respond to HRW. ALLEGED
    • Late 2025: Zambia permitted Hua Yu Sheng to take control of most of Kabwe's lead waste for zinc extraction. WEB HRW op-ed. Sayari has no entity record, so ownership is UNVERIFIED
    • 5 Nov 2025: appeal hearing in the Anglo American class action. WEB BHRRC
  4. Epoch 4 · 2026

    African Union child-rights complaint

    • 16 Apr 2026: IHRDA files an African Union child-rights complaint against Zambia. WEB
    • 20 Oct 2026: the committee was scheduled to consider the complaint. Outcome: UNVERIFIED (not in the data pack).

Extraction tonnage by year is not charted: the data pack has no such series. Only the ~6.4 million tonne remaining-waste figure is available.

End products: where zinc goes

Global zinc use in 2024 by sector. This is a global breakdown; the data pack cannot say what share of it comes from Kabwe waste. WEB ILZSG via Natural Resources Canada

Galvanizing (protective zinc coating on steel) is the largest use by a wide margin. HRW lists zinc as a key metal in wind turbines and also names solar and hydro. WEB HRW No firm-level link from Kabwe to any specific product is documented in the pack.

Open question

Does Kabwe zinc reach high-voltage semiconductors or fusion-reactor coils?

No documented link found. No source in the data pack connects Kabwe zinc to high-voltage semiconductors, fusion-reactor coils or Helion Energy. This is a question to test, not a finding. UNVERIFIED

Context only: Helion has a 2023 power purchase agreement with Microsoft for its Orion plant in Malaga, WA (50 MW by 2028), and a reported Series G valuation of US$15.5bn. WEB neimagazine.com That is Helion's own valuation, not revenue from Kabwe zinc.

Policy proposal: Decentralized Cryptographic Provenance Ledger (DCPL)

The authors' proposal, not evidence. It has not been piloted, and the trade data show flows crossing several borders.

  1. Automated hash verification: every raw payload crossing a border generates a distributed token linked to a verified local Environmental Impact Assessment (EIA).
  2. Market metric reversal: unverified minerals are kept out of international exchange pricing tiers.
  3. Smart-contract supply locks: purchasing pipelines require traceability from extraction origin to deployment.

Caveats

  • Kabwe's poisoning is mainly lead. Zinc is the co-product that makes waste reprocessing profitable.
  • Sayari flows from Zambia are largely transit via Tanzania and include DRC-origin concentrate. They cannot be attributed to Kabwe waste.
  • Sayari figures are record counts, not tonnage or value. Entity records are fragmented.
  • ILAB flags are country/product-level risk indicators, not evidence of child labour at any firm.
  • Mine start year differs by source (1902 / 1904 / 1906), so a range is shown.
  • The Anglo American claims are alleged and disputed. Sales to Datong Industries, Chengde Mining and Superdeal Investments are alleged by miners and unanswered by the companies.
  • The US$224–593M social cost is a secondary-source estimate. Scavenger pay is a single 2016 account.
  • Helion's US$15.5bn valuation is its own valuation, not revenue from Kabwe zinc.
  • The DCPL is a policy proposal and has not been piloted.

  • The end date of ZMERIP.
  • The outcome of the African Union committee's consideration, scheduled for 20 Oct 2026.
  • Refiner margins, component-maker revenue and per-product zinc value.
  • Scavenger pay from 2025–2026. The earlier "$1–2/day child wage" has been removed as unsourced.

  • "Global zinc demand rises 80% by 2050."
  • "Children absorb 5× as much lead as adults" and maternal bone-lead mobilisation: standard toxicology, not checked here.
  • Removed as unsourced: CHIPS Act subsidies, "trillions in net-zero valuations", galvanizing "for maritime wind turbines", and the claim that Zambia overrode its own environmental law.
